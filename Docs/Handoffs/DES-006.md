## Findings
- **Root cause:** the slice never uses the ported parser. `ShiftSim.Search` calls `Recommender.Recommend` with 1 of 6 family crit/neg sets (`ShiftSim.cs:136-146`, `:505-506`). Nothing in `Simulation/` or `Presentation/` calls `QueryParser`, `Responder`, `Refine` or `Similarity`. Customer lines contain exactly one parser keyword (`SliceIntents.json`).
- **PS1b as written** adds questions and workflows, but its composer turns structured KNOWN fields into wording by itself (GAME-017 D5, UX-005 §4). The game does the interpreting, not the player.
- **Missing tool steps:** the Refine follow-up chips (`tool.html` L19583-19641) are in no packet; the chip *selection* is not ported. Also missing: wrong-term handling / Help (L16227-16229, L16677), strain lookup + nearest-in-stock (L16541-16554), like-X (L16047-16050), the New/Experienced toggle (L1585-1589, L14827), role tags / why line (L16253-16264), and the budget-only effect prompt (L16662-16667).
- **The tool defines the interpretation step itself:** its Training "Customer language" cards give phrasings → meaning → script → follow-up questions (L2196-2319).
- Similar, Compare and Details are covered well by GAME-018.

## Recommendation
Customers get a TRUE **need** (`trueQuery` in tool vocabulary, golden-captured) and speak in 7 styles. The player reads each "said card" as `Their words` or a budtender term ("Note it as…"). The query is the joined readings → `QueryParser.Parse(Game)` → `Responder.Respond`. Composed cards beat free typing for PS1b: gateable, deterministic, controller-friendly, and no kitchen-sink autopilot (RDA §3.3).

Overall, the game is looking positive and you’ve done excellent work running it. Seeing the actual assets come into the environment is making a huge difference. The deli jars especially look amazing.

My current feedback:

**Controls / UX**
- Controls feel clunky and awkward right now. WASD itself is mostly fine; my bigger issue is the camera movement and how looking/turning feels.
- Esc should always open a pause/menu screen.
- Doors need to be addressed so movement through the store feels natural.

**Environment / Layout**
- The jars look excellent. The nugs still look off, but that is not a priority yet.
- I hope the outside world becomes much more lively over time.
- Some rooms still appear to be mapped incorrectly, especially the HUB, although this may simply not be finished yet.
- I’m slightly concerned about the office. With the current placeholder density it is almost unwalkable, so we need to be careful about fitting the final assets while maintaining believable walking/work space.
- The south-side registers are essentially unusable right now because there is not enough room to access and operate them. The entire register layout needs another audit against the actual store/reference footage.
- The deli shelving looks beautiful, but it currently feels unrealistically full. Also, I believe the real setup has more than one deli wall—closer to three—so that should be checked against the references.
- Everything currently feels very uniform and exact. The finished store should feel more lived-in and operational: slight variation in placement, partially used supplies, objects left in believable places, uneven stocking, cleaning items, personal items, etc.

**Register / Workstation Detail**
Each register should eventually have:
- label printer
- receipt printer
- scanner
- mouse
- keyboard, even though touchscreen is used for most interactions
- storage beneath the register for bags, boxes, personal items, cleaning supplies, etc.

Small details like a spray bottle and rag would help a lot. A spray bottle hanging from one of the scale shelves would be particularly believable.

**Customer Flow**
- The customer transaction flow felt strange because the customer was already waiting at my register. I did not call them over.
- Customers should move through the actual entry/ID-check flow. Right now there is no security guard checking IDs and customers appear to bypass that area entirely.
- Calling the next customer should feel like part of the player's workflow rather than customers automatically appearing at a register.

**Customer Interaction / Recommendation Gameplay**
This is probably my biggest concern from the current demo.

The actual transaction was too mundane: choose one effect, choose one strain, done.

I gave you the baseline for a genuinely strong recommendation tool, and I do not want that depth lost when it becomes a game mechanic. The TFS recommendation system should be an important part of the customer experience rather than being reduced to a basic selection menu.

Customer interactions should eventually be significantly deeper. Customers should communicate needs in different ways, the player should have to interpret what they actually want, ask useful follow-up questions where appropriate, and then use the recommendation workflow to make a good choice.

The game should preserve the feeling of actually being a knowledgeable budtender, not just selecting an effect from a menu.

**TV / Advertising Wall**
When the TV wall is built out, it should cycle through dispensary-style product advertisements. Something like a new ad every 5–10 seconds would make sense.

**Long-Term Parking Lot / Customer Throughput**
I think the parking lot could eventually become an actual gameplay system rather than just exterior decoration.

Our parking lot is comically small, and one of the real operational problems is getting customers through the store quickly enough to keep cars moving. When we’re busy, you either need the employees on the floor to move faster or you need more employees actively serving customers.

If transactions start taking too long, the parking lot can turn into a madhouse.

I’d eventually like customer traffic inside the store to be reflected outside:
- arriving customers occupy actual parking spaces
- customers leaving free those spaces
- busy periods can fill the lot
- additional customers may have to wait or circle when there is nowhere to park
- throughput inside the store directly affects parking turnover
- staffing levels and transaction speed therefore have consequences beyond just the indoor queue

There is also a real problem where someone parks along the side of the building and makes it difficult or impossible for other cars to back out. Meanwhile, people can be circling the block waiting for a space to become available.

That could create a really interesting feedback loop:

customer volume → parking congestion → pressure to move customers through efficiently → staffing/transaction decisions → cars leaving → new customers gaining access.

It would make busy periods feel much more like actually operating this location rather than simply having a longer indoor customer queue.

This is definitely a longer-term system and not something I think needs attention immediately, but I think it could become an important part of the store simulation.

**Longer-Term Character / Start Setup**
I think a character setup/customization screen would add a lot. Long term, it could let you:
- customize your character
- choose a starting role
- select difficulty
- potentially influence your starting experience/progression


IDEAS 

Store condition over the day: The store should visibly change as a shift progresses—counters get messier, jars empty out, bags and boxes accumulate, trash fills, supplies move around, cleaning gets skipped, and closing should look noticeably different from opening.

Break room / downtime: The break room should feel occupied and lived in, with employees eating, sitting for a minute, talking, leaving drinks or personal items around, or briefly escaping a bad rush. Mostly ambient, but it would make coworkers and the store feel much more real.

Neighborhood activity: The exterior should feel connected to an actual neighborhood—cars coming and going, delivery drivers, pedestrians, people waiting outside, traffic, nearby business activity, weather, and other small changes so the dispensary feels like it exists in a living world rather than an isolated game map.

One other long-term idea: I’d like the game to have some grounded, deadpan humor without turning corny or feeling like it is constantly trying to tell jokes. Ideally the comedy comes naturally from the simulation—awkward customers, bad parking, equipment failing at the worst time, weird requests, clutter, staffing problems, or situations escalating into something ridiculous but still believable. The characters and game should mostly play these moments straight, so the humor feels observational and earned rather than forced.

Another long-term idea would be making coworkers feel like actual people rather than generic NPC workers. Different employees could have their own strengths, weaknesses, habits, personalities and work styles—someone might be extremely fast but messy, great with customers but slow, disappear into the back too often, excel at deli, hate register, or be the person you rely on during a rush. That could naturally feed into scheduling, breaks, call-outs, training, morale, promotions, delegation and eventually management gameplay, while also creating a lot of the grounded humor I mentioned above.

These do not need to be immediate, but I think it fits the game well.

Overall I’m happy with the direction. The environment is starting to feel real, especially when the higher-quality assets appear. My main priorities from this demo would be improving controls, fixing store/layout accuracy, making customer flow believable, and substantially deepening the transaction/recommendation experience.
